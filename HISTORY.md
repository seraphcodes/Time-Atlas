# TimeAtlas: Rome, AD 100 — historical status of every landmark

Snapshot: **AD 100, third year of Trajan's reign.** Trajan is in Rome; the First Dacian War starts in 101. Pliny the Younger delivers his *Panegyricus* this year.
Researched 25 November 2026 (session 1). Rule: anything not standing in AD 100 is either left out or shown with a clear "later" label.

Status: **Standing** · **Building** (under construction or restoration in 100) · **Gone** (destroyed before 100) · **Later** (not yet built).
Evidence: **Documented** (remains plus texts give plan and size) · **Inferred** (location known, form partly reconstructed from parallels, coins or plans) · **Invented** (we filled a gap; disclosed on screen).

## How we label the rest
- Colour on temples and façades: **Inferred** from surviving paint traces and Pompeii; exact schemes **Invented**.
- Crowds, markets, laundry, awnings: **Invented**, built to fit what Roman texts describe (Juvenal *Satire* 3, Martial).
- Street layout outside the excavated centre: **Invented** to fit the hills, the Marble Plan where known, and major roads (Vicus Longus, Argiletum, Vicus Tuscus, Sacra Via, Clivus Suburanus).

## Rome today (session 8)

The "Today" switch shows the same ground as it is now. The AD 100 city becomes a faint outline; what survives is drawn solid in weathered stone; what was built after AD 100 is drawn in blue-grey. Only the Forum valley, the Colosseum valley, the Velia and the Circus have a modelled "today"; elsewhere the AD 100 monuments stay as outlines, and the grey blocks outside the archaeological park stand in for the modern city (Invented, disclosed).
